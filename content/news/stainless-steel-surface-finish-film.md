  en:
    - "A fabricator in Vietnam rejected three coils of 8K mirror sheet last month. The film peeled off clean — no adhesive transfer, no bubbles, no scratches. But under a 45-degree inspection light the surface showed a faint milky haze in the exact shape of the film. The customer called it a film shadow. Nothing was wrong with the adhesive chemistry. The film was simply too matte for a surface that reflects most of the light hitting it."
    - "Stainless surface finish is not cosmetic. 2B, No.4 and 8K differ in roughness by more than an order of magnitude, and roughness controls how much of the adhesive layer actually touches metal. 2B — cold rolled, annealed, pickled and skin-passed — typically sits around Ra 0.1 to 0.3 micrometres. No.4 brushed sheet measures roughly Ra 0.5 to 1.5 micrometres with a clear directional grain. 8K mirror is polished below Ra 0.05 micrometres, which is why it reflects an image instead of scattering light."
    - "Contact area is the variable most buyers never calculate. On a brushed surface the film touches only the peaks of the grain and bridges the valleys. On mirror sheet it touches almost the entire surface. Same adhesive, same coat weight, yet peel force measured to GB/T 2792 or ASTM D3330 method A can differ by 30 to 50 percent between the two. A film that behaves perfectly on 2B will feel aggressively tacky on 8K — and aggressive tack is the first step toward residue."
    - "There are two separate mechanisms behind a dulled surface, and they need different fixes. The first is adhesive micro-flow: under coil tension and warehouse heat, adhesive creeps into the grain valleys of brushed sheet, then anchors there. When you peel, fragments stay in the valleys and scatter light. The second is film haze. A PE film with high internal haze acts like a diffuser; leave it on a mirror panel for eight weeks in a warm warehouse and the surface reads dull even though nothing has transferred."
    - "That distinction matters because the two failures look identical in a rejection photo. If the haze disappears after wiping with isopropyl alcohol, it is surface contamination from the film plasticiser or from condensation. If it survives wiping and shows up as a faint pattern, adhesive has migrated into the grain. Only the second case is a real adhesion mismatch."
    - "Picking the right film comes down to three questions, in this order. First, what is the surface roughness — this sets the adhesion tier. Second, what happens to the sheet after filming: laser cutting, bending, deep drawing, or just storage and shipping — this sets the temperature and tensile requirements. Third, how long is the sheet in the film: two weeks domestically is a different problem from twelve weeks in a sea container crossing the equator."
    - "For roughness matching, a practical starting point is three tiers. Mirror and 8K surfaces normally call for a low-tack tier, typically 5 to 15 grams per 25 millimetres measured by GB/T 2792 at 180 degrees and 300 millimetres per minute. 2B and BA surfaces tolerate a medium tier, roughly 15 to 30 grams per 25 millimetres. Brushed No.4 usually wants the medium-to-high tier because the peaks carry the load, but the adhesive must be soft enough to wet the grain without flowing into it. Treat these as starting points, not specifications — always confirm on your own sheet."
    - "Verification is cheap and takes four tests. Peel adhesion to GB/T 2792 at 180 degrees gives you the baseline number. A residue test holds the filmed panel at 65 degrees Celsius for 24 hours, cools it, then peels and inspects under raking light. A humidity test runs 40 degrees Celsius at 90 percent relative humidity for 72 hours, which is the closest bench proxy for a sea container. A UV exposure test of 100 hours catches the films that go brittle before they reach the customer."
    - "Application discipline decides the rest. Apply below 40 degrees Celsius — above that the adhesive is already mobile and you trap it in the wrong place. Keep web tension low; a film stretched on application will retract and lift at the edges within days, and edge lift is often misdiagnosed as weak adhesive. On brushed sheet, apply along the grain direction so the squeegee roller pushes adhesive down into valleys instead of dragging it sideways. On mirror, use a hard rubber roller and two passes, because a single pass leaves micro-bubbles that read as haze."
    - "One more correction worth making: edge lift is rarely a sign that the film is too thin. It is almost always tension, surface temperature, or a contaminated edge from shear cutting. Thickening the film to fix lift usually makes the residue problem worse, because a heavier film applies more peel force at the same adhesion level. If you are fighting lift on 8K, check tension and edge cleanliness before you ask for a thicker product."
    - "Blue PE film with a controlled low-tack adhesive is the practical answer for mixed production lines, because the colour makes missed patches obvious during inspection and the low tack keeps mirror surfaces clean. The full specification for [GDY-031 blue PE protective film](/products/GDY-031) covers width, thickness and adhesion tiers. If you are evaluating three finishes at once, request a [free sample set](/free-sample) with 2B, No.4 and 8K panels from your own stock and we will match the adhesion tier to each one."
    - "Concretely: cut three 200 by 300 millimetre panels from the same coil you are currently running, label the finish and the mill direction, and send them with your current film sample and the rejection photo if you have one. We return a written recommendation with the adhesion tier, thickness and application tension for each finish, plus peel data measured on your own panels rather than on our reference steel."

    - "上个月越南一家加工厂退了三卷 8K 镜面板。撕膜过程很干净：没有残胶、没有气泡、没有划伤。但在 45 度检验灯下，板面上出现一层淡淡的乳白色雾状痕迹，形状和膜完全一致。客户管它叫「膜影」。胶水配方没出问题，问题在于这卷膜的哑度配不上一块能反射大部分入射光的表面。"
    - "不锈钢表面等级不是外观问题。2B、No.4、8K 三者的粗糙度差了不止一个数量级，而粗糙度直接决定胶层真正接触金属的面积。2B 是冷轧后退火酸洗再平整，Ra 通常在 0.1 到 0.3 微米；No.4 拉丝板 Ra 大约 0.5 到 1.5 微米，带有明显方向性纹理；8K 镜面抛光到 Ra 0.05 微米以下，所以它反射的是影像，不是散射光。"
    - "接触面积是绝大多数采购从来不计算的变量。拉丝面上，膜只压住纹理的波峰，波谷是架空的；镜面上，膜几乎贴满整个表面。同样是那卷膜、同样克重的胶，用 GB/T 2792 或 ASTM D3330 方法 A 测出来的剥离力能差 30% 到 50%。在 2B 上表现正常的膜，到了 8K 上就会显得「太黏」——而太黏正是残胶的第一步。"
    - "表面变哑有两个完全不同的机理，修法也不同。第一种是胶层微流动：在卷取张力和仓库高温作用下，胶慢慢爬进拉丝面的波谷并锚定在那里，撕膜时碎胶留在谷里，光一照就散。第二种是膜本身的雾度高，PE 膜像一层柔光镜；镜面板在温暖仓库里压上八周，即使一点胶都没转移，表面看上去也是哑的。"
    - "这个区分很关键，因为两种失效在退货照片里长得一模一样。用异丙醇擦一下：雾痕消失的，是膜里增塑剂或冷凝水造成的表面污染；擦不掉、还带隐约纹路的，才是胶迁移进了纹理。只有第二种才是真正的粘力错配。"
    - "选膜的顺序是三个问题。第一，表面粗糙度是多少，这决定粘力档位；第二，贴膜之后这道板要经历什么——激光切割、折弯、深冲，还是仅仅存放和运输，这决定耐温和拉伸要求；第三，膜要在板上待多久，国内两周和漂洋过海十二周是两个完全不同的问题。"
    - "粗糙度匹配可以先用三档起步。镜面和 8K 一般走低粘档，按 GB/T 2792、180 度、300 毫米每分钟的条件，典型值在 5 到 15 克每 25 毫米；2B 和 BA 面能承受中粘档，大约 15 到 30 克每 25 毫米；拉丝 No.4 通常要中高粘，因为承力的是波峰，但胶必须软到能润湿纹理却不能流进去。这些是起点不是标准，一定要在自己的板上复验。"
    - "验证很便宜，四个测试就够。GB/T 2792 的 180 度剥离给基线数据；残胶测试把贴好膜的板放进 65 摄氏度烘 24 小时，冷却后撕膜，用斜射光看；湿热测试 40 摄氏度、90% 相对湿度跑 72 小时，这是海运集装箱最接近的台面模拟；再加 100 小时紫外老化，能筛掉那些还没到客户手上就发脆的膜。"
    - "施工纪律决定剩下的一半。贴膜时板温控制在 40 摄氏度以下，超过这个温度胶已经开始流动，会被压到不该去的地方。收卷张力要低：贴的时候拉得越紧，膜回弹越厉害，几天内边角必然翘起，而翘边经常被误判成胶太弱。拉丝面顺着纹理方向贴，让刮板把胶压进波谷而不是横向拖；镜面用硬胶辊压两遍，一遍过会留下读起来像雾气的微气泡。"
    - "还有一个常见的误判要纠正：翘边很少说明膜太薄，绝大多数是张力、板温或者剪切边带油污造成的。为了治翘边去加厚膜，通常会让残胶更严重，因为同样粘力下厚膜的剥离力更大。8K 上遇到翘边，先查张力和边缘清洁度，再考虑换厚度。"
    - "对混线生产的工厂，蓝色 PE 膜配合可控低粘胶是最实际的方案：蓝色让漏贴的地方在检验时一眼看见，低粘则保证镜面不被污染。[GDY-031 蓝色 PE 保护膜](/products/GDY-031) 的完整规格里列了幅宽、厚度和粘力档位。如果你手上同时有三种表面要评估，直接申请[免费样品](/free-sample)，用你自己库存的 2B、No.4、8K 板各切一片寄过来，我们按表面等级分别配档。"
    - "具体做法：从你现在正在跑的那卷料上切三片 200 乘 300 毫米的样板，标好表面等级和轧制方向，连同你现在用的膜样一起寄过来；如果有退货照片也一并附上。我们回一份书面建议，逐种表面给出粘力档位、厚度和施工张力，剥离数据全部测在你自己的板上，不拿我们的标准钢板充数。"
