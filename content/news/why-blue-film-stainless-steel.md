  en:
    - "Every week a buyer asks us some version of the same question: is blue film better than clear film? The honest answer is that colour does not make a film stronger, thicker or more heat resistant. What it changes is how your line sees the surface — and on stainless steel, seeing the surface is most of the battle."
    - "**Clear film disappears. That is the problem.** On brushed No.4 or a mirror 8K sheet, a transparent PE film applied correctly is nearly invisible under factory lighting. Operators cannot tell at a glance whether a sheet is still covered, whether the film has lifted at an edge, or whether a roll has been double-wrapped. Blue film makes the covered area unmistakable, which is why it became the default on stainless lines even though it costs slightly more than natural PE."
    - "**Contrast helps inspection, not just handling.** A blue background raises the visual contrast of bubbles, gel particles, dust and coating streaks. Inspectors working at line speed catch defects faster on blue than on clear, especially under mixed lighting where clear film produces glare. One appliance-panel plant we supply moved its final visual check from clear to blue film and cut missed bubble defects noticeably — not because the film was better, but because the defects were easier to see."
    - "**Colour is a free batch-control tool.** Many plants run two or three surface grades on the same line: 2B, No.4 and 8K. Using a different film colour per grade, or per alloy batch, means a mixed stack is visible before it reaches the customer. This is the cheapest mistake-proofing available — it costs nothing beyond the masterbatch, and it survives shift changes and staff turnover."
    - "**What colour does not change.** The pigment sits in the PE carrier layer at roughly 1–3% by weight; it does not enter the adhesive layer in a properly co-extruded film. So adhesion, peel strength and residue behaviour are set by the adhesive system and the film thickness, not by the colour. If a supplier tells you blue film is stronger, ask for the peel data. It will be the same as their clear grade at the same thickness."
    - "**When blue is the wrong choice.** Mirror-finish 8K surfaces inspected under strict lighting are easier to read through clear film, because blue tints the reflection and can mask fine haze or micro-scratch. UV-curing processes need light transmission. And some appliance customers specify no colour in the film because a tint can read as contamination on a white or light-grey panel. Match the colour to the inspection method, not to habit."
    - "**The real risk with coloured film is pigment migration.** Low-grade masterbatch can bleed under heat and humidity, leaving a faint blue haze on the steel after removal — which is far more expensive than the film itself. Ask for a migration test at 40°C and 90% RH for 72 hours on your actual substrate, and check the sheet under daylight, not just under factory LEDs. A good supplier will run this before you place a volume order."
    - "**How to specify it.** For stainless sheet and coil in our PE range we work with thickness from 0.02 to 0.20 mm and peel strength from 30 to 300 g/in, measured by 180° peel after 24 hours dwell at 23°C and 50% RH. Thin 0.03–0.05 mm film suits short-cycle in-house handling; 0.08 mm and above suits coil storage, transport and export packing where puncture resistance matters more than conformability."
    - "**Run a three-roll comparison before you commit.** Take one roll of clear, one of light blue and one of dark blue from the same production batch. Apply them to the same substrate, store them under your real conditions for your real dwell time, then peel and inspect under the same light your customer uses. Compare three things: how easily defects were seen while covered, how clean the peel was, and whether any tint remained. That test answers the colour question better than any spec sheet."

Tell us your substrate, surface finish and dwell time, and we will match the thickness and peel grade to your process. Both colours come from our [PE blue protective film](/products/GDY-031) range, so the only variable in your test is the colour."

    - "**透明膜会消失，这就是问题所在。** 在拉丝 No.4 或镜面 8K 板上，贴得好的透明 PE 膜在车间灯光下几乎看不见。操作工没法一眼判断这张板还有没有膜、边缘有没有翘起、这卷是不是重复包了两层。蓝膜让覆盖区域一目了然，所以即使比本色 PE 略贵，它还是成了不锈钢产线的默认选择。"
    - "**对比度帮的是质检，不只是搬运。** 蓝色底衬会把气泡、晶点、灰尘和涂布条纹的视觉对比度拉高。在产线节拍下，质检员在蓝膜上发现缺陷的速度比透明膜快，尤其是在混合光源下——透明膜会产生反光干扰。我们供货的一家家电面板厂把终检从透明膜换成蓝膜后，漏检气泡明显减少。不是膜变好了，是缺陷更容易被看见。"
    - "**颜色是零成本的防错工具。** 很多厂同一条线要跑 2B、No.4、8K 两三个表面等级。按等级或按合金批次用不同颜色的膜，混料在到达客户之前就能被发现。这是最便宜的防错手段——除了色母粒几乎不增加成本，而且不怕换班和人员流动。"
    - "**颜色不改变什么。** 在结构合理的共挤膜里，颜料留在 PE 载体层，添加量大约 1–3%（重量比），不会进入胶层。所以粘力、剥离力和残胶表现由胶系和膜厚决定，不由颜色决定。如果供应商说蓝膜更粘，直接要剥离力数据——同厚度下，它和透明膜是同一个数。"
    - "**什么时候不该用蓝色。** 镜面 8K 在严格光源下检查时，透过透明膜看反射更容易判断，因为蓝色会给反射上色，可能掩盖细微雾化和微划伤。UV 固化工艺需要透光。还有部分家电客户明确要求膜不带颜色，因为在白色或浅灰面板上，任何色调都可能被当成污染。颜色要匹配检验方式，而不是匹配习惯。"
    - "**彩色膜真正的风险是颜料迁移。** 低档色母粒在高温高湿下会析出，撕膜后在不锈钢上留下一层淡淡的蓝雾——这个损失远比膜本身贵。要求供应商在你实际用的基材上做 40°C、90% RH、72 小时的迁移测试，并且在日光下看板，不能只在车间 LED 下看。靠谱的供应商会在你下大货单之前主动做这一步。"
    - "**参数怎么定。** 我们 PE 系列用于不锈钢板和卷材的常规范围是厚度 0.02–0.20 mm、剥离力 30–300 g/in，测试条件为 23°C、50% RH 下贴合 24 小时后做 180° 剥离。0.03–0.05 mm 的薄膜适合厂内短周期周转；0.08 mm 以上更适合卷材存放、运输和出口包装，因为这时抗穿刺比服帖更重要。"
    - "**下结论前先做三卷对比。** 从同一生产批次里取一卷透明、一卷浅蓝、一卷深蓝，贴在同一基材上，按你真实的存放条件和贴膜时长放置，然后在客户实际使用的光源下撕膜检查。只看三件事：带膜时缺陷好不好发现、撕下来干不干净、有没有留色。这个测试比任何规格书都更能回答颜色问题。"
