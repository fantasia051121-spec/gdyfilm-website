  en:
    - "A mirror-finish 8K sheet can take hours of polishing and seconds to ruin. The film arrives like any other roll, gets laminated, sits in a warehouse or a container for weeks, and peels off cleanly — until the day it does not. Under the customer's inspection light the sheet comes back marked: fine haze across the reflection, or a web of micro-scratches too shallow to feel with a fingernail. Both defects come from the protective film, and both are preventable once you stop treating mirror stainless as just another stainless surface."
    - "**Mirror steel punishes everything rough.** A polished 8K surface is typically specified around Ra 0.06 µm, far smoother than a No.4 brushed finish, so any particle harder than the steel becomes a cutting edge and any aggressive adhesive becomes sandpaper. When a roll is laminated with line tension over dust, or the adhesive layer has lifted grain from the master roll, contact points carry the whole load. Under a bright lamp held at an angle, those points read as micro-scratches even though nobody ever touched the steel with a tool."
    - "**Scratch and haze are two different failures.** Scratch is mechanical: something harder than the surface moves across it under pressure. Haze is chemical or optical: a faint uniform cloud in the reflection, caused by adhesive transfer, plasticizer or pigment migration out of the film, or reactions with residues left on the steel. A thicker film will not fix haze; a softer adhesive will not fix scratch. Diagnose first — run a fingernail across the mark. A scratch is a groove you can feel; haze is not. Then choose the film for the failure you actually have."
    - "**Controlling scratch: thickness and tack, in that order.** For stainless sheet and coil, the working PE range is 0.02 to 0.20 mm thick with peel adhesion from 30 to 300 g/in, measured as 180° peel after 24 hours dwell at 23°C and 50% RH under GB/T 2792 / ASTM D3330 conditions. For a mirror finish, specify the low end of adhesion: 60–120 g/in is a typical starting band, because a flat surface needs no aggressive tack to hold. Films below roughly 0.05 mm also conform better, spreading contact over dust instead of bridging on it."
    - "**Controlling haze: clean adhesive and a migration check.** Haze almost always traces back to the adhesive system — low-molecular-weight components that migrate onto the steel, especially under heat and humidity. Before committing to a volume order, condition the film on your actual mirror sheet at 40°C and 90% RH for 72 hours, then peel and judge the reflection under daylight. Prefer a clear, pigment-free film on 8K: masterbatch is one more migration source, and a blue tint also hides early haze that a clear film would reveal while the sheet can still be re-polished."
    - "**What to ask a supplier for — three documents, not a datasheet.** First, peel curves at several dwell times, not one number, because adhesion grows as the film sits: a grade reading 100 g/in at 24 hours can read several times that after months indoors. Second, the migration or residual-adhesion result at the 40°C / 90% RH / 72 h condition, run on mirror steel rather than glass. Third, the shelf-life limit of the film itself — expired PE film is one of the quietest causes of haze on stored coil."
    - "**Lamination and storage decide half the outcome.** Apply the film with moderate tension so it lies flat without stretching; an over-stretched film recovers on the roll and lifts at the edges, letting dust underneath. Keep laminated sheets out of direct sun — a container wall reaching 50°C combined with high humidity is the worst case the migration condition is meant to predict. And set a removal deadline: as a typical range, low-tack PE films are specified to peel cleanly within six months of indoor storage; beyond that, require the residual-adhesion limit in writing."
    - "**The decision rule for mirror 8K.** A low-adhesion, pigment-free PE film around 0.05 mm thick, peel grade near 60–120 g/in at the 24-hour test condition, a written 72-hour migration result on your own substrate, and a removal deadline matched to your logistics. One sheet-level test beats any datasheet: laminate two offcuts from your own line, keep one at room temperature and one in a warm, humid corner for two weeks, then peel both and compare the reflections."

    - "一张 8K 镜面板，抛光要几个小时，报废只要几秒钟。膜和其他订单一样到货、贴膜、进仓库或集装箱放几周，撕下来通常干干净净——直到某一天不是。客户的检验灯一打，板面上是反射里的淡淡雾化，或者指甲摸不出来、灯下看得见的一整片微划伤。这两种缺陷都来自保护膜，而且都可以防住，前提是不再把镜面板当成普通不锈钢来选膜。"
    - "**镜面会惩罚一切粗糙的东西。** 8K 抛光面的典型指标在 Ra 0.06 µm 上下，比 No.4 拉丝面光滑得多。任何比钢更硬的颗粒都会变成刀刃，任何剥离力偏高的胶都会变成砂纸。产线带着张力贴膜时，如果板面有灰、胶层带进了母卷的浮纤，接触点会承受全部载荷。检验灯斜着打过去，这些点就是微划伤——哪怕从头到尾没人用工具碰过这块钢。"
    - "**划伤和雾化是两类失效，先分清再选膜。** 划伤是机械问题：比表面更硬的东西在压力下划过表面。雾化是化学或光学问题：反射里一层均匀的淡雾，来自胶的转移、增塑剂或颜料从膜里迁移，或者膜与钢面残留物反应。加厚膜治不了雾化，换软胶治不了划伤。先用指甲划过痕迹判断：能摸出沟的是划伤，摸不出的雾化是另一回事。按你实际遇到的失效去定参数。"
    - "**防划伤：先定厚度，再定粘力。** 不锈钢板和卷材用 PE 膜的常规范围是厚度 0.02–0.20 mm、剥离力 30–300 g/in，测试条件为 23°C、50% RH 下贴合 24 小时后做 180° 剥离（GB/T 2792 / ASTM D3330）。镜面建议从粘力下限起步：60–120 g/in 是常见的起始档，因为平面不需要靠高粘力固定。厚度 0.05 mm 以下的膜服帖性也更好，能把接触摊开到灰尘周围，而不是架在灰尘上。"
    - "**防雾化：胶要干净，迁移要验证。** 雾化几乎都能追到胶系——低分子量成分在热和湿气下往钢面迁移。下大单之前，把膜贴在你的实际镜面板上做 40°C、90% RH、72 小时的条件处理，撕膜后在日光下看反射。8K 建议用无色透明膜：色母粒本身就是多出来的迁移源，而且蓝色底色会掩盖早期雾化，等透明膜能看出来的阶段板子还来得及返抛。"
    - "**向供应商要三样东西，而不是一张数据表。** 第一，多个贴膜时长下的剥离曲线，不是单个数字——粘力随存放时间增长，24 小时读 100 g/in 的档位，室内放几个月后可能是几倍。第二，40°C / 90% RH / 72 小时条件下的迁移或残余粘力结果，要在镜面钢上做，不是玻璃上。第三，膜本身的保质期上限——过期 PE 膜是库存卷材出现雾化最容易被忽略的原因。"
    - "**贴膜和存放决定了另一半结果。** 贴膜张力适中，让膜平铺不拉伸；拉过头的膜会回缩、边缘翘起，把灰尘卷进膜下。贴好的板不要暴晒——集装箱壁到 50°C 加上高湿度，正是迁移测试条件要模拟的最坏情况。还要定一个撕膜期限：作为典型范围，低粘 PE 膜按室内存放六个月内撕净来标定；超过这个时间，要求供应商书面确认残余粘力上限。"
    - "**镜面 8K 的选膜规则。** 低粘、无色的 PE 膜，厚度 0.05 mm 上下；24 小时测试条件下剥离力落在 60–120 g/in；拿到书面 72 小时迁移结果，而且是在你自己的基材上做的；撕膜期限与你的物流周期匹配。任何数据表都不如一次板级测试：从你的产线取两片边料，一片放室温，一片放温暖潮湿的角落两周，之后同时撕膜对比反射。"
