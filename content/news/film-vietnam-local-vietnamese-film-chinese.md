  en:
    - "A purchasing manager in Binh Duong puts two quotes side by side for the film that protects his aluminium profiles: one from a Vietnamese converter who can deliver tomorrow, one FOB from a Chinese factory at a lower price per kilogram. He is about to make the most common mistake in this market — comparing the two on price alone, as if they were the same product arriving by different routes. They are not. Local converting stock and factory-direct import film answer different problems, and each wins in a different part of your operation."
    - "Start with the arithmetic that per-kilogram comparison hides. PE film is sold by weight because it is made by weight: polyethylene has a density of roughly 0.92 g/cm³, so every 0.01 mm of thickness adds about 9.2 grams per square metre. A 0.05 mm film therefore weighs around 46 g/m² and a 0.10 mm film around 92 g/m² — double the material for the same visible area. If the local quote is based on a heavier construction, its cheaper per-kilo number can actually cost you more per square metre. Convert everything to price per square metre at a stated thickness before comparing anything else."
    - "Where local stock genuinely wins: speed and small quantities. A converter holding jumbo or slit rolls in a local warehouse can deliver within a day or two, in lots as small as a single pallet, with no customs paperwork and no container minimum. For emergency top-ups, trial runs and short orders that change width every fortnight, that responsiveness has real value — a line stoppage waiting on a ship costs far more than the price difference between the two quotes."
    - "Where factory-direct wins: range and adjustment. Most Vietnamese converters do not coat their own film; they buy jumbo rolls and slit them, so the specification range they can sell is whatever the mother roll happened to be. A coating factory can vary the parameters that matter: peel strength engineered anywhere in the 30–300 g/25mm band measured to GB/T 2792, thickness anywhere from 0.02 to 0.20 mm, and widths from 150 up to 2100 mm — the figures on a construction-grade PE film, but the principle generalises. The more awkward your substrate or process, the more the adjustable range is the answer."
    - "There is also a traceability difference. Film that passes through a converter carries two production events — the coating batch and the slitting lot — and the buyer usually sees only the second. When adhesion drifts on the third order, a direct factory can trace the coating batch, the adhesive formulation and the curing record in one system; a middleman can only ask his own supplier, and the trail goes cold there. The second-order quality drop that buyers complain about is usually a chain problem, not a factory problem."
    - "Then the items quotes leave out: ocean freight plus customs clearance makes the import route a matter of weeks rather than days, capital sits in stock sized to the MOQ, and resolving a claim across a border costs time. A defective local roll is swapped this week; a defective imported roll opens a negotiation measured in shipping cycles. An importer should price that delay, not just the ocean freight."
    - "A workable decision rule: if monthly consumption does not fill a container, or your widths change often, buy local stock and pay for flexibility. If consumption is large and steady, and the substrate is particular — mirror stainless, powder-coated panels, oiled galvanizing — buy factory-direct and have the adhesive matched, because generic middleman stock is exactly where residue and edge-lift complaints come from. Many Vietnamese plants end up doing both: one stable direct-import specification for the main line, local stock as a buffer."
    - "Whichever route you pick, decide it with data from your own line, not the datasheet. Run the baseline 180-degree peel to GB/T 2792 at 23 degrees Celsius and 50 percent relative humidity, then the industry damp-heat cycle of 40 degrees and 90 percent RH for 72 hours, then peel on your actual substrate and inspect for residue. In a climate like Vietnam's, two films that look identical at 23 degrees routinely separate into winner and loser after the damp-heat cycle. Test both candidates on the same day, with the same operator and the same panels."
