  en:
    - "A Tier-2 press shop in Rayong runs cold-rolled and galvanised sheet, typically 0.7 to 1.2 mm, into door reinforcements and brackets. Parts leave the press with the film still on, sit in a humid warehouse for two to three weeks, then move to welding and painting. Two failures surface at the strip station: the film has cracked at deep-drawn corners and lifted at trimmed edges, or the adhesive has built up enough to leave a ghost line the paint line rejects. Both start with specifying a general-purpose film for a stamping process, not with the price per square metre."
    - "Stamping is not flat handling. Under the blank holder the sheet stretches locally, the die radius rubs the coating, drawing oil sits between steel and film, and a trimmed edge leaves a burr that catches the carrier. A film needs enough **elongation** to follow the draw without tearing, enough tack to stay put through the press, and an adhesive that does not climb during storage. These demands pull against each other: higher tack resists edge lift but raises residue risk, and a thicker carrier survives the draw yet conforms less well over a burr. The right point is a compromise, not the maximum of one number."
    - "Start from the real parameter window of a stamping-grade PE film. The [PE home-appliance protective film](/products/GDY-071) is listed at 150–2100 mm width, 200–1000 m roll length, 0.02–0.20 mm thickness and 30–300 g/25mm peel strength, with few gel points, good elongation and minimal tack growth. Treat these as **typical ranges under stated test conditions**, not a promise for your line: a 0.05 mm film at low tack behaves differently from a 0.08 mm film near the top, and only a trial on your actual blank confirms where your process sits."
    - "Match the specification to the operation instead of buying one grade for every part. Blanking and piercing need clean edges and a film that does not string at the shear; bending needs elongation so the carrier does not split across the radius; deep drawing needs the most stretch and the gentlest release. Substrate matters as much as the press: galvanised, cold-rolled and PCM/VCM colour-coated steel sit at different points of the 30–300 g/25mm window, and an oiled blank needs an adhesive that bonds through the oil film. One datasheet cannot pick that point."
    - "Verify with a standard method on your own material. GB/T 2792 and ASTM D3330 both describe a **180-degree peel test**; the usual reference condition is 23 ± 2 °C with 50 ± 5 % relative humidity, and GB/T 2792 specifies a 300 mm/min crosshead speed after a defined dwell. Run one set after a short dwell and a second after the longest storage your parts see — two to three weeks in a Thai warehouse, or longer for export. ISO 9227 covers salt-spray corrosion of the metal, not peel behaviour of the film, so it answers a different question."
    - "Local conditions widen the gap between a datasheet and the press. EEC plants in Chonburi, Rayong and Chachoengsao run JIT schedules with little buffer, so film is often applied and stamped in the same shift, then stored at ambient heat; a shop floor around 35 °C with high humidity is a normal summer condition, not an edge case. Adhesive systems build tack faster under heat, so a grade that peels cleanly at 23 °C in a supplier's lab may behave differently after weeks on a warm rack. Quote every number as a typical range with its test conditions, then confirm it on the real part."
    - "Choose carrier colour as a quality gate, not a cosmetic decision. [Blue PE film](/products/GDY-031) makes edge lift, mis-application and contamination visible at a receiving dock, which matters when a Tier-1 audits incoming stampings. For painted or colour-coated blanks where the film must survive punching and stamping, the [stamping-grade PE film](/products/GDY-071) is the closer starting point, and its printable version supports up to 4 colours for part numbering and orientation marks. Colour coding also stops a mixed-pallet mistake reaching the press."
    - "Write the specification in this order before asking for a quote: name the substrate and its surface state, oiled or dry, galvanised or coated; list every downstream operation the film must survive; state the longest dwell and the highest storage temperature; then set a peel window and a thickness. The [construction-grade PE range](/products/GDY-033) is a useful reference for wider panels at 150–2100 mm and 30–300 g/25mm, but a window is not a target — the target comes from your own peel and forming trial. Keep one alternative grade qualified on the line, so a supply interruption becomes a switch, not a line stop."
    - "Start with a sample, not a price list. Request the exact width, thickness and tack level you intend to order, ask for the roll to be cut from a production batch, and run one full stamping-and-storage cycle on your own blank: apply, press, store for the real dwell, peel at 180 degrees, then inspect under your paint or welding acceptance criteria. [Free samples](/free-sample) are the standard entry point — state the substrate, the press operations, the drawing oil and the storage temperature so the sample matches the order. Fix commercial terms only after the part passes."
