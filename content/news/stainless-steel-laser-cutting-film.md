  en:
    - "The complaint arrives with a photo: a 3 mm stainless sheet, cut on a fiber laser, film still on. The cut edge has a brown halo about one millimetre wide, and where the operator peeled the film, a ring of charred adhesive came away with it. The cut itself is dimensionally perfect. The part is scrap anyway, because the halo will show through powder coating and the customer will not accept it."
    - "This is not a film defect and it is not a laser defect. It is a thermal mismatch. PE protective film softens at roughly 105 to 130 degrees Celsius and degrades above 150. The cut zone in a fibre laser reaches well over 1,500 degrees Celsius. The film is not being cut — it is being cooked, and the heat-affected zone spreads 0.1 to 0.5 millimetres beyond the kerf depending on power and speed."
    - "The mechanism is worth understanding because it explains every parameter choice that follows. The laser couples energy into the metal, the metal conducts heat laterally, and the film sitting on top absorbs that heat through contact. Three things then happen in sequence: the adhesive softens first, the PE carrier shrinks back from the kerf, and any adhesive inside the heat-affected zone carbonises. That carbonised ring is what you see as the halo, and it is what transfers to the sheet when the film is peeled."
    - "Assist gas makes it worse before it makes it better. High-pressure nitrogen clears molten metal from the kerf efficiently, but it also turbulently pushes hot spatter sideways under the film edge. On thin sheet below 2 millimetres, lowering gas pressure to 8 to 12 bar and raising cutting speed usually produces a cleaner edge than maximising pressure, because the sheet spends less time at temperature."
    - "Material choice dominates everything else. PE film is the worst common option for laser cutting because its melting range sits exactly in the temperature band the heat-affected zone passes through on its way down. PET film, with a melting point around 250 to 260 degrees Celsius, survives noticeably better and is the usual choice for laser-cut stainless. PVC film should never be used: it contains roughly 57 percent chlorine by weight, and laser cutting releases hydrogen chloride, which attacks the cut edge and corrodes the machine's extraction path."
    - "Thickness is counter-intuitive here. Buyers often specify a thicker film thinking it protects better. In laser cutting the opposite holds: a 0.08 millimetre PE film has less thermal mass and shrinks away from the kerf faster than a 0.12 millimetre film, which keeps a larger volume of adhesive in the hot zone for longer. Going thicker typically widens the halo rather than protecting the surface."
    - "The practical parameter window for 1 to 3 millimetre stainless on a fibre laser, with 0.05 to 0.08 millimetre film, looks like this: power 1.5 to 3 kilowatts, cutting speed 4 to 8 metres per minute, nitrogen assist at 10 to 14 bar, focus at or slightly below the surface. The goal is minimum line energy — the highest speed that still produces a clean kerf. Every minute spent at lower speed is extra heat conducted sideways into the film."
    - "There are four process routes and each has a different failure mode. Cutting without film avoids the halo entirely but invites scratches during handling, which on a No.4 or 8K surface is a worse problem. Cutting with film accepts a controlled halo and relies on post-cut cleaning. Cutting first and filming afterwards means handling bare polished sheet, which usually costs more in rework than the film saves. Waterjet cutting has no heat-affected zone at all and is the right answer for thick plate or mirror finish where any discolouration is unacceptable."
    - "Acceptance criteria need to be written down before the job runs, not argued about afterwards. A workable standard for most fabricators: discolouration confined within 1 millimetre of the cut edge, no molten bead or raised residue, and complete removal of charred adhesive after peeling with no ghost mark under raking light. Anything beyond that is a reject, and if the reject rate exceeds 5 percent the parameter window is wrong, not the film."
    - "Post-cut handling decides whether a marginal cut becomes a good part. Peel within 24 hours — adhesive that has been through a thermal cycle continues to cross-link and will leave more residue the longer it stays. Peel at 180 degrees, slowly, at a shallow angle rather than ripping upward. Then wipe the edge with isopropyl alcohol within the hour, before the carbonised ring oxidises into the surface texture. On brushed sheet, wipe along the grain, not across it."
    - "One more failure worth naming: some shops run PVC film because it is cheap and clings well, then wonder why the cut edge rusts within a week and the extraction duct corrodes. That is hydrogen chloride from the chlorine content, and it is a chemical problem no parameter change will fix. If a supplier cannot tell you the base resin of a protective film, do not put it under a laser."
    - "For mixed production where the same line cuts 2B, No.4 and occasional mirror sheet, a thin blue PE film with controlled low tack handles the handling protection well, but it is the wrong choice for the cut itself — use it on parts that are cut before filming, or switch to PET for the laser operation. The full range is listed under [GDY-031 PE protective film](/products/GDY-031); if you are qualifying a laser process, request a [free sample set](/free-sample) in both PE and PET and run the same job on both before you commit to a specification."

    - "机理值得讲清楚，因为它解释了后面每一个参数选择。激光把能量耦合进金属，金属横向导热，贴在上面的膜通过接触吸收这部分热。接下来依次发生三件事：胶层先软化，PE 基材向切缝方向回缩，落在热影响区里的胶被碳化。那圈碳化就是肉眼看到的黄晕，也是撕膜时转移到板面上的东西。"
    - "辅助气体这件事，加压不一定更好。高压氮气能把熔融金属从切缝里吹干净，但同时会把高温飞溅紊流地推到膜边底下。2 毫米以下的薄板，把气压降到 8 到 12 bar、同时提高切割速度，边缘通常比一味加大气压更干净，因为板材在高温里停留的时间更短。"
    - "材质选择比其他所有因素都重要。PE 膜是激光切割里最差的选择，因为它的熔点区间恰好落在热影响区降温时经过的那一段。PET 膜熔点大约 250 到 260 度，扛得住得多，是激光切割不锈钢的常用选择。PVC 膜绝对不能用：它含氯约 57%，激光切割会释放氯化氢，腐蚀切口，也腐蚀设备的抽风管路。"
    - "厚度这件事是反直觉的。采购往往指定更厚的膜，觉得保护更好。激光切割恰好相反：0.08 毫米的 PE 膜热容小，比 0.12 毫米的膜更快从切缝边缘回缩，留在高温区里的胶反而更少。加厚通常让黄晕更宽，而不是更好地保护表面。"
    - "1 到 3 毫米不锈钢、0.05 到 0.08 毫米膜厚，在光纤激光上的实用参数窗口大概是这样：功率 1.5 到 3 千瓦，切割速度 4 到 8 米每分钟，氮气 10 到 14 bar，焦点落在板面或略低于板面。目标是线能量最小——在不影响切口质量的前提下取最高速度。速度每降一档，都是更多热量横向传进膜里。"
    - "工艺路线有四条，失效模式各不相同。不带膜切，完全没有黄晕，但上下料过程必然划伤，在 No.4 或 8K 面上这是更严重的问题。带膜切，接受一圈可控的黄晕，靠切后清洁解决。先切后贴，意味着要裸手处理镜面板，返工成本通常高于省下的膜钱。水刀没有热影响区，厚板或者镜面完全不能接受变色的场合，这才是正解。"
    - "验收标准要在开工前写下来，不要等出了事再吵。多数工厂可用的标准是：变色限制在切口 1 毫米以内，不允许熔珠和凸起残留，撕膜后不留焦胶印，斜射光下看不到鬼影。超出即判不合格；如果不合格率超过 5%，那是参数窗口错了，不是膜错了。"
    - "切后处理决定一次勉强合格的切割能不能变成好零件。24 小时内撕膜——胶经历过一次热循环之后会继续交联，留得越久残胶越多。撕的时候 180 度、慢速、贴面小角度，不要向上猛扯。然后在一小时内用异丙醇擦边缘，赶在碳化圈氧化进表面纹理之前。拉丝面顺着纹理擦，不要横着擦。"
    - "还有一种失效值得点名：有些厂用 PVC 膜，因为便宜、贴得牢，然后纳闷为什么切口一周内就生锈、抽风管道也腐蚀。那是含氯带来的氯化氢，化学问题，改任何参数都救不回来。如果供应商说不清一卷保护膜的基材树脂是什么，就不要把它放到激光下面。"
    - "混线生产、同一台设备既要切 2B 又要切 No.4、偶尔还有镜面板的场合，薄型蓝色 PE 膜配可控低粘，在搬运防护这一段表现很好，但它不适合跟着进激光——要么用在切后再贴的工序，要么激光工序换成 PET。完整规格在 [GDY-031 PE 保护膜](/products/GDY-031) 页面；如果你正在验证激光工艺，申请一份 [PE 和 PET 的对比样品](/free-sample)，用同一个零件、同一组参数各跑一遍，再定规格。"
